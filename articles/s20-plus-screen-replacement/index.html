Samsung Galaxy S20+ Screen Replacement Guide

Updated 2026-10-02·Screen Replacement & Repair·~770 words

Replacing a Samsung Galaxy S20+ screen means fitting a new curved Dynamic AMOLED 2X display with a built-in ultrasonic fingerprint reader, a job that demands care around both the fingerprint sensor and the curved-edge adhesive seal. Genuine Samsung parts and various grades of aftermarket panel are both available, with real differences in how closely they match the original screen's look and feel.

Key takeaways

  • The S20+ uses a curved Dynamic AMOLED 2X display with an in-screen ultrasonic fingerprint sensor, meaning fingerprint reliability depends on the quality and compatibility of the replacement screen used.
  • Genuine Samsung AMOLED parts preserve original colour accuracy and fingerprint sensor function most closely, while lower-tier aftermarket panels can show dimmer brightness, colour shifts, or inconsistent fingerprint recognition.
  • The curved edges of the S20+ display make a clean adhesive reseal more technically demanding than on a flat-screen phone.
  • As with other Samsung flagships of this era, the screen is glued in and contributes to water resistance, so proper resealing with fresh adhesive matters for keeping that protection intact.
  • The S20+ is ageing enough now that genuine Samsung parts may be harder to source than for newer models, which can push buyers toward OEM-grade aftermarket options.

What makes the S20+ screen replacement distinct?

The Galaxy S20+'s Dynamic AMOLED 2X panel has a curved edge design and an ultrasonic fingerprint sensor built into the display itself, rather than a separate capacitive sensor elsewhere on the phone. This combination means the replacement screen has to be both optically and electronically compatible — a mismatched or lower-quality panel can make fingerprint unlock unreliable even if the rest of the display looks and feels fine.

OEM vs aftermarket options for this model

Genuine Samsung AMOLED service parts give the closest match to the phone's original brightness, colour, and fingerprint sensor performance. As the S20+ ages, genuine stock can be less readily available than it was when the phone was newer, which has increased reliance on OEM-grade aftermarket panels — these aim to replicate Samsung's specifications closely but can vary between suppliers. Budget aftermarket screens are the most likely to show visible differences: dimmer peak brightness, a cooler or warmer colour cast, or occasional fingerprint sensor inconsistency.

It's worth asking directly which tier of screen a repairer is quoting, and whether they test the in-display fingerprint sensor before returning the phone.

Why the curved edges and adhesive matter

Unlike a flat-screen phone, the S20+'s curved edges make it harder to achieve a perfectly flush, well-sealed fit during reassembly. The adhesive used to secure the screen also contributes to the phone's resistance to dust and moisture, so a rushed job that skips a full adhesive reseal — or reuses old adhesive — can leave the edges prone to lifting over time and reduce water resistance.

What affects the cost of this repair

Cost depends on the part tier used (genuine Samsung AMOLED vs OEM-grade vs budget aftermarket), whether the in-display fingerprint sensor is preserved and tested, and labour time for the curved-edge adhesive reseal. Because pricing varies between repairers and shifts as genuine parts become scarcer for this ageing model, it's worth checking a current, itemised price list for your exact model rather than relying on an old quote.

DIY vs professional repair

DIY replacement is possible but more technically demanding than it looks, mainly because of the embedded fingerprint sensor and the curved adhesive seal. A poor DIY job risks a non-functional fingerprint reader, lifted edges, or reduced water resistance. Given the phone's continuing value and the sensor complexity involved, most owners get better, more reliable results from a professional repair that tests fingerprint function and properly reseals the device.

Frequently asked questions

Does the Galaxy S20+ fingerprint sensor work after a screen replacement?

It should if a compatible, good-quality screen assembly is fitted and tested, but lower-grade aftermarket screens can make the in-display ultrasonic fingerprint sensor less reliable or non-functional.

How much does it cost to replace a Samsung S20 Plus screen?

Cost depends on the part tier chosen and labour, and varies by repairer — check a current, itemised price list for your specific model for an accurate figure.

Is the S20+ still water resistant after a screen repair?

Only if the display is resealed with fresh adhesive during the repair; reused or poorly applied adhesive reduces the phone's original resistance to dust and moisture.

Can I replace my Galaxy S20+ screen myself?

It's possible but more demanding than older or flat-screen Samsung models due to the curved edges and embedded fingerprint sensor, so many owners prefer a professional repair to avoid sensor or sealing issues.
