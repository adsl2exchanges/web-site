iPod Repair Near Me: What's Still Possible in 2026

Updated 2026-10-02·Finding a Local Repair Shop·~810 words

Apple discontinued the entire iPod lineup in May 2022, so there's no official Apple repair program or new genuine parts supply for any iPod model anymore — but that doesn't mean repair is impossible. Independent repairers and refurbishers can often still fix batteries, screens and charging issues using third-party or salvaged parts, depending on the model.

This guide explains what's realistically fixable on an iPod today, what to expect from a repairer, and when it makes more sense to retire the device.

Key takeaways

  • Apple stopped selling iPods in 2022 and does not offer official repair for them
  • Independent repairers rely on aftermarket or salvaged parts, since new genuine iPod parts are no longer manufactured
  • Battery replacement is the most common and most worthwhile iPod repair, since lithium-ion batteries degrade regardless of use
  • Older iPod models (classic, nano, shuffle) have very limited part availability compared to the iPod touch
  • Weigh repair cost against the iPod's sentimental or practical value, since parts scarcity can make some repairs harder to justify

Does Apple still repair iPods?

No. Apple discontinued the iPod touch — the last model still sold — in May 2022, ending the iPod product line entirely after more than two decades. Apple's official repair and parts programs generally wind down for a product a number of years after it's discontinued, and iPods are now well into or past that window for most models, meaning Apple Stores typically can't service them even if you wanted to go that route.

This makes independent, third-party repairers the realistic option for anyone wanting to keep an iPod running today.

What can still be repaired on an iPod?

The most common and most worthwhile repair is the battery. Every iPod uses a lithium-ion battery that degrades over time regardless of how carefully it's been used, and a battery swap can bring an old iPod back to reliable daily use. Screens (on iPod touch and iPod nano models with displays) can often be replaced using aftermarket parts. Charging port and headphone jack issues are also commonly repairable, particularly on iPod classic and iPod touch models where these are more robust mechanical components.

What's much harder to repair reliably is a logic board fault or storage failure on older iPod classic models, since specialised components for these are increasingly scarce.

Where do parts come from if Apple doesn't make them anymore?

Independent repairers source iPod parts from two main places: aftermarket manufacturers who still produce compatible batteries and screens for popular models (iPod touch and iPod classic in particular), and salvaged parts taken from other non-functional iPods (sometimes called harvested or pulled parts). Quality varies between suppliers, so it's reasonable to ask a repairer where their parts come from and whether they offer any warranty on the specific component.

Because supply is finite and not being replenished by new manufacturing from Apple, parts for less common iPod models can become harder to find over time.

Is it worth repairing an old iPod?

This comes down to what the iPod means to you. If it holds a music library you value, has sentimental history, or you simply prefer a dedicated music player without notifications and apps, a battery or screen repair is often a small investment for years of extra use. If the device needs multiple repairs at once (battery, screen and charging port all failing together), it's worth weighing the total repair effort against simply replacing it with a current device or a newer secondhand iPod.

Because iPod repair costs depend heavily on model, part availability and the repairer's sourcing, check a current, itemised price list or get a direct quote rather than assuming a fixed cost.

How do you find a repairer who still works on iPods?

Search specifically for repairers who list iPod or "legacy Apple device" repair, since not every modern phone repair shop keeps iPod tools and parts on hand anymore. Ask directly whether they have experience with your specific iPod generation — an iPod classic's hard-drive-based design is very different internally from a flash-storage iPod touch or nano.

A repairer who's upfront about part sourcing and realistic about what can and can't be fixed is generally more trustworthy than one who promises an easy fix for every fault.

Frequently asked questions

Can I still get an iPod fixed?

Yes, independent repairers can often still fix batteries, screens and charging issues on most iPod models using aftermarket or salvaged parts, even though Apple no longer offers official repair.

Does Apple still sell iPods?

No, Apple discontinued the entire iPod lineup, including the iPod touch, in May 2022.

Why won't my old iPod hold a charge?

This is almost always due to normal lithium-ion battery degradation over years of charge cycles, and is typically fixable with a battery replacement.

Is it worth fixing an iPod classic in 2026?

It depends on the fault — battery and charging repairs are usually worthwhile, but storage or logic board issues can be harder to fix reliably due to scarce specialist parts for this older design.
