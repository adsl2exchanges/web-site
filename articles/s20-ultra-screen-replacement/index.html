Samsung Galaxy S20 Ultra Screen Replacement Guide

Updated 2026-10-02·Screen Replacement & Repair·~760 words

Replacing a Samsung Galaxy S20 Ultra screen means fitting a new curved AMOLED display and digitizer assembly, and the curved edges are the main thing that make this repair more involved than a flat-screen phone — the glass wraps around the sides of the device, which affects both how it's handled during the repair and how much the part typically costs. It's a well-established repair, but one where part quality and installation care matter more than usual.

As one of Samsung's larger, higher-spec flagship phones, the S20 Ultra also carries some of the more complex display technology found in the Galaxy S lineup, including a high refresh rate panel.

Key takeaways

  • The S20 Ultra uses a curved-edge AMOLED display, which is generally costlier and trickier to replace than a flat screen.
  • Curved glass is more delicate at the edges, so careful handling during removal and fitting matters more than on flat-screen phones.
  • The S20 Ultra's high refresh rate (up to 120Hz) display is more advanced technology than standard 60Hz panels, which can affect part cost.
  • Genuine Samsung AMOLED parts generally offer the most accurate colour and touch response, though quality aftermarket options exist.
  • As the phone ages, genuine parts can become harder to source compared to current Samsung flagships.

What makes the S20 Ultra's screen different to replace

The Galaxy S20 Ultra uses a curved AMOLED display where the glass wraps around the left and right edges of the phone rather than sitting completely flat. This curved design means the screen assembly is more delicate at the edges, since the glass is thinner and more exposed to stress at the point where it curves.

During replacement, this means extra care is needed when removing the old screen and seating the new one, as misalignment or excess pressure at the curved edges is more likely to cause cracking than it would be on a flat panel. It's one of the reasons curved-screen repairs are generally considered more technically demanding than flat-screen equivalents.

The S20 Ultra also has a large 6.9-inch display with a high resolution and up to 120Hz refresh rate, both of which reflect more advanced (and costlier) display technology than Samsung's smaller or older models.

How curved AMOLED affects cost and part choice

Curved-edge AMOLED screens like the one on the S20 Ultra generally cost more than flat AMOLED or LCD screens for two reasons: the panel itself is more complex to manufacture, and the installation requires more careful, time-consuming handling.

Genuine Samsung parts are generally the safest choice for preserving the curved display's colour accuracy and touch response all the way to the edges, where quality aftermarket alternatives are more likely to show inconsistencies — slightly less accurate colour, or touch sensitivity that's a little less reliable right at the curve. Budget aftermarket curved screens in particular can be prone to this.

Because the S20 Ultra is a few years old now, it's worth asking a repairer directly whether they're fitting a genuine Samsung panel or a quality aftermarket alternative, and what the trade-offs are for your specific unit.

What affects S20 Ultra screen replacement cost

As with any phone, cost is driven by part type (genuine vs aftermarket), the complexity of the curved display, and labour time. The S20 Ultra's large, high refresh-rate curved AMOLED panel sits toward the more expensive end of Samsung's range to repair, reflecting both the technology involved and the extra care curved glass requires during fitting.

Part availability also plays a role — as a now older flagship, genuine Samsung parts for the S20 Ultra may be less readily available than for current-generation Galaxy S models, which can affect both price and wait times. Because pricing moves regularly and varies by repairer, it's worth checking a current, itemised price list for the S20 Ultra specifically.

Is it worth repairing an S20 Ultra screen vs upgrading?

The S20 Ultra was a genuine flagship when released, with strong specifications that still hold up reasonably well. If the phone is otherwise in good condition — healthy battery, no other damage — a screen repair is generally a sensible way to keep using a capable device.

If the battery has also degraded significantly, or the phone is starting to show its age in everyday performance, it's worth weighing the screen repair cost against how many more years of useful life and software updates the phone realistically has left before committing.

Frequently asked questions

Is it harder to replace a curved screen like the S20 Ultra's?

Yes, curved AMOLED screens are generally more delicate and technically demanding to replace than flat screens, since the glass is thinner at the curved edges and more prone to damage if handled imprecisely during fitting.

Does the S20 Ultra's screen cost more to replace than a regular S20?

Typically yes, the S20 Ultra's larger, higher-resolution curved display with a higher refresh rate generally uses more advanced and costlier technology than the standard S20's screen.

Can you get aftermarket screens for the Samsung S20 Ultra?

Yes, quality aftermarket curved AMOLED screens are available for the S20 Ultra, though they can vary somewhat in colour accuracy and edge touch sensitivity compared to genuine Samsung parts.
