How to Fix a Cracked Smartphone Screen

Updated 2026-10-02·Screen Replacement & Repair·~830 words

If your smartphone screen is cracked, the first priority is stopping the damage from getting worse: cover any shattered glass with clear tape or a screen protector, avoid pressing on the crack, and back up your data as soon as possible in case the touchscreen stops responding. A genuine fix almost always means professional or DIY screen replacement — there's no way to make cracked glass or a broken display whole again.

Key takeaways

  • A cracked screen won't repair itself and no product actually 'heals' broken glass — temporary fixes only protect you and the phone until a proper replacement is done.
  • Covering the crack with clear packing tape or a tempered glass screen protector straight away reduces the risk of cuts, dust ingress, and the crack spreading further.
  • Back up your phone immediately after a crack, since a worsening display or dead touchscreen can make this much harder later.
  • A DIY screen protector patch is a reasonable short-term measure but is not a substitute for replacing a genuinely broken display or digitizer.
  • Avoid DIY full screen replacement if your phone has Face ID, in-display fingerprint sensors, or is still under warranty, since these add risk and can void coverage.

What to do immediately after cracking your screen

As soon as you notice a crack, stop using the phone normally if glass is visibly lifting or shattered — sharp edges can cut fingers, and continued pressure can spread the crack further across the panel. Wipe away any loose glass fragments carefully, then cover the area with a layer of clear packing tape or, better, a tempered glass screen protector, which holds the fragments in place and keeps dust and moisture out.

If the touchscreen still responds, back up your photos, contacts, and important data straight away. Cracked screens can deteriorate quickly, including losing touch response entirely, so don't assume you'll have time to do this later.

Temporary protection: tape and screen protectors

A simple, effective temporary fix is to apply a screen protector — either a basic plastic film or a tempered glass protector — directly over the cracked screen. This won't make the crack disappear or restore a shattered display's clarity, but it does three useful things: holds broken glass fragments together, reduces the chance of cuts, and slows moisture or dust getting into the crack and causing further damage like discolouration or dead pixels.

Clear packing tape works as a stopgap if you don't have a screen protector handy, though it's less durable and can leave a sticky residue over time.

DIY screen protector patch vs proper repair

It's important to be clear about what a screen protector patch actually achieves: it manages the cracked glass, it does not fix the screen. The display underneath is still physically damaged, and if the crack has reached the digitizer (the touch-sensing layer), touch accuracy can still degrade over time even with a protector in place.

A proper repair means either replacing the full screen assembly yourself with a repair kit, or having a professional do it. This is the only way to restore the phone to full working condition — clear display, accurate touch, and no risk of glass shards.

When DIY repair makes sense (and when it doesn't)

DIY screen replacement can be a reasonable option on older phones with simple, well-documented repairs and cheap, widely available parts. It's less advisable when:

  • Your phone has Face ID or an in-display fingerprint sensor, where precise handling of sensors is required.
  • The phone is still under manufacturer warranty or has coverage through insurance, since opening it yourself can void that cover.
  • You don't have the right tools — using the wrong screwdriver or prying tool is a common cause of further damage.
  • The phone has water resistance you want to preserve, which depends on correctly resealing the device with fresh adhesive.

If any of these apply, a professional repair is usually the safer route.

What affects the cost of a proper screen repair

Cost is driven by the device model and age, the type of display (basic LCD vs OLED vs high-refresh panel), whether you choose a genuine/OEM part or a budget aftermarket one, and labour complexity for that specific phone. Because prices vary a lot between devices and change over time, it's worth checking a current, itemised price list for your exact model rather than estimating from a figure you've seen for a different phone.

How to avoid future cracks

A case with raised edges and a decent screen protector are the two cheapest, most effective ways to reduce the chance of a crack from an everyday drop. Tempered glass protectors are generally more crack-resistant than plastic film, and a case that extends slightly past the screen's surface helps stop the glass from taking a direct hit when dropped face-down.

Frequently asked questions

How do I fix a cracked smartphone screen myself?

For a true fix, you need to replace the display assembly, either with a DIY repair kit matched to your exact phone model or through a professional repairer — covering the crack with tape or a screen protector only protects it temporarily and doesn't restore the display.

How do I fix a broken display on my phone temporarily?

Apply a tempered glass screen protector or clear packing tape over the cracked area to hold glass fragments in place and reduce further damage, and back up your data as soon as possible in case the touchscreen stops working.

Can a cracked phone screen fix itself or heal over time?

No, cracked glass and damaged display layers don't repair themselves, and the crack can spread further or cause touch and display problems to worsen the longer it's left unrepaired.

Is it safe to use a phone with a cracked screen?

It's not dangerous in most cases, but shattered glass can cut fingers, and a cracked screen is more vulnerable to moisture and dust getting into the display, which can cause further damage over time.
