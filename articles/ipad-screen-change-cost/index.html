iPad Screen Change Cost: What Actually Drives It

Updated 2026-10-02·Screen Replacement & Repair·~830 words

The cost of changing an iPad screen depends mainly on which iPad model you have, whether just the glass or the full glass-and-digitizer assembly needs replacing, and whether genuine Apple parts or quality aftermarket parts are used. There's no single fixed price across all iPads because these factors change so much between models and repair situations.

Rather than quoting a figure that goes out of date quickly, it's more useful to understand exactly what pushes the price up or down for your specific iPad.

Key takeaways

  • iPad model is the single biggest cost driver — Pro models with larger, laminated displays generally cost more to fix than entry-level iPads.
  • On most iPads the glass and digitizer are fused into one assembly, so even a small crack usually means replacing the whole front panel.
  • OEM (genuine Apple) parts typically cost more than quality aftermarket parts but preserve features like True Tone more reliably.
  • iPads with Face ID or advanced ambient light sensors can lose certain features if repaired without proper part-pairing or calibration.
  • Checking a current, itemised price list for your exact iPad model is the only reliable way to know what a repair will cost, since prices move often.

What determines the cost of an iPad screen change?

Four main factors drive iPad screen repair pricing: the specific model, the screen technology it uses, whether genuine or aftermarket parts are fitted, and the labour involved in opening that particular design.

Model matters because Apple has used different display and digitizer designs across the iPad range — a basic iPad (like the 6th, 7th, 8th or 9th generation) is generally simpler and cheaper to repair than an iPad Pro, which uses a larger, laminated display with more advanced technology such as ProMotion or mini-LED on some versions.

Part sourcing also matters. A part for a current, widely-sold model is generally easier and cheaper to obtain than one for an older or less common iPad, where stock may be limited and pricing less competitive.

Glass-only crack vs full digitizer replacement

On almost all modern iPads, the front glass and the digitizer (the touch-sensing layer) are fused into a single assembly rather than being separate, replaceable layers. This means that even if only the outer glass is cracked and the display underneath still works perfectly, the whole front panel assembly typically needs to be replaced as one unit.

This is different from some older or more basic devices where glass-only replacement was more common. For iPads, it's safest to assume that any cracked glass will mean replacing the complete screen assembly, which is reflected in how repairers price the job.

The practical upshot is that a tiny chip in the corner of an iPad's glass usually costs the same to fix as a fully shattered screen, since the same assembly is being replaced either way.

OEM vs aftermarket iPad screens: what differs

Genuine Apple (OEM) parts are made to the exact original specification and are generally the most reliable choice for preserving full functionality, including accurate colour reproduction and True Tone behaviour where applicable. They typically come at a premium over aftermarket alternatives.

Quality aftermarket screens can be a reasonable middle ground — noticeably cheaper than genuine parts while still functional — but they can vary in colour accuracy, touch sensitivity, and long-term durability depending on the manufacturer. Budget aftermarket parts at the very low end are more likely to have visible quality issues like poor viewing angles or less precise touch response.

On iPad models with Face ID or sophisticated ambient light and True Tone sensors built into the display assembly, non-genuine parts or a repair done without proper calibration can cause True Tone to stop working correctly or trigger onscreen warnings, even though the screen itself functions normally.

Does iPad screen repair cost vary by generation and model?

Yes, noticeably. Entry-level iPads (6th through 9th generation and similar) tend to sit at the more affordable end because their screens use simpler technology and are produced in very high volumes, keeping part supply strong.

iPad Air and iPad mini models sit in the middle, while iPad Pro models — especially those with larger 12.9-inch displays, ProMotion refresh rates, or mini-LED backlighting — are generally the most expensive to repair, reflecting the more advanced and costly display technology involved.

Because Apple periodically changes internal design even within a single iPad family, it's also worth confirming your iPad's exact generation before assuming a quoted part will fit, since visually similar models can use different internal components.

How to get an accurate price for your iPad screen repair

Because pricing depends so heavily on your specific iPad generation, screen type, and part choice, the only reliable way to get an accurate figure is to check a current, itemised price list against your exact model — generic prices quoted online for "an iPad screen" are rarely meaningful across such a wide product range.

When comparing quotes, ask specifically whether the price includes a genuine or aftermarket part, whether True Tone and Face ID functionality (if applicable) are expected to work normally afterwards, and what warranty is offered on the repair. These details affect the real value of a quote far more than the headline number alone.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to replace an iPad screen?

It depends on the iPad model, whether genuine or aftermarket parts are used, and local labour rates — there's no single fixed cost, so it's best to check a current, itemised price list for your exact iPad generation.

Is iPad screen repair more expensive than iPhone screen repair?

It can be, mainly because iPad screens are physically larger and some models use more advanced display technology, both of which generally push parts and labour costs higher than a typical iPhone screen.

Can you just replace the glass on an iPad without replacing the whole screen?

In almost all modern iPads the glass and digitizer are fused together, so a cracked glass typically means replacing the full screen assembly rather than just the outer layer.

Does iPad screen replacement cost differ between generations, like the 9th gen vs iPad Pro?

Yes — entry-level iPads generally use simpler, more widely available display technology and tend to be more affordable to repair than iPad Pro models with larger or more advanced screens.
